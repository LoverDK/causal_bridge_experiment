\subsection{Matched-certificate weight and geometric-bound ablations}
We conducted a post-review attribution analysis on the same 1,800 synthetic worlds as the workflow experiment. Each world shares calibration data, proxy noise, and source-specific trial streams across variants. We fix either a random or nearest-proxy bridge order, so all variants receive the same source prefix at each budget. Under each of the two certificates separately, we compare optimized, uniform, inverse-variance, and nearest-source weights. All weights use design information only. We also optimize each geometric bound separately and evaluate all bounds at the same weights. Fixed-budget scores and first-release stopping costs are reported separately.

At tolerance 0.3, optimized $R_2$ weights improve release over uniform weights by 79.67 percentage points, with a paired 95\% bootstrap interval of [77.00,82.33], and save 123.84 participants, interval [119.36,128.32]. These are exploratory individual intervals from 2,000 world-paired resamples stratified by response surface. At the common final budget, their respective all-target MAEs are 0.0334 and 0.1405, and their mean certificate widths are 0.4224 and 0.7061. Thus the difference is not solely a consequence of evaluating different stopping times. The supported menu provides a boundary: optimized, uniform, and inverse-variance $R_2$ weights all release without acquisition, and uniform weights have slightly lower MAE. No variant releases on the unreachable menu.

Across all tested surfaces, support geometries, schedules, certificates, and tolerances, taking the minimum of the two bounds does not improve release probability or stopped cost over the barycentric bound alone. At identical weights the Lipschitz bound sometimes tightens the radius on nonlinear surfaces, mainly in unreachable geometries, without changing release decisions. These results support the role of weighting and barycentric geometry in this DGP; they do not establish an additional practical benefit from taking the minimum. All evaluated policies have zero observed bad releases and full empirical path coverage in this run. These conservative empirical results do not imply universal or conditional coverage guarantees. Complete cell summaries, fixed-budget scores, saved weights, and paired comparisons are retained with the experiment artifacts.
