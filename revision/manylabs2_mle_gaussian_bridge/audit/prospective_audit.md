# Independent prospective statistical audit

Prepared before inspecting any simulation results. The criteria and decisions in the final section were frozen by the root agent before the main simulation. Earlier recommendations remain recorded for provenance and are superseded where the frozen decisions differ. No held-out target outcomes were opened. The only observed outcomes allowed to this audit are the source-only data. The outcome-free design inventory contains 7,228 participants across 57 sites; 2,863 rows are sealed-target design rows and 4,365 are source rows.

## Estimation and failure taxonomy

For positive profile weight p_c, a cell with n_tc=0 makes its probability unidentified in the saturated model. Do not assign it zero, an arbitrary 0.5, a pooled probability, or a smoothed probability and call the result an MLE. Retain that site-replication as a missing-estimator failure. A structurally inactive category p_c=0 contributes zero and should be skipped before division; it is not an estimation failure. The source-only identity-summary model may use pooled category contrasts, but the requested site-level diagnostics must cover all 57 sites.

For n_tc>0, all failures or all successes have a well-defined boundary maximizer qhat in {0,1}; the associated finite logistic coefficient does not exist. Separate finite-logit MLE success, boundary/extended probability MLE availability, empty-cell identifiability, and invalid standard-error events. An extended probability MLE may support unconditional Bernoulli decision-risk evaluation. It does not restore regular asymptotic normality. Jeffreys smoothing is allowed for prespecified DGP generation, not as an unlabelled repair of a failed per-site MLE.

Every site/scenario/scale retains its full planned Monte Carlo denominator. Successful-only summaries must state their success probability and cannot stand in for unconditional performance. Wald coverage treats undefined intervals as noncoverage and reports invalid-interval frequency separately. Report the all-12-candidates validity probability for joint diagnostics; an average of 12 marginal success rates is not joint success. If future counts simply multiply historical category-arm counts, historical empty cells persist forever; any reallocation must be prospectively defined and preserve site composition and arm ratios to the declared integer tolerance.

## Suggested adequacy gates

These are practical, prespecified descriptive tolerances, not a finite-sample theorem. For each site/scenario/scale: finite-MLE success at least 99%; absolute oracle-standardized mean error at most 0.10; empirical-to-oracle variance ratio in [0.90,1.10]; absolute skewness at most 0.30; absolute excess kurtosis at most 0.50; maximum normal-quantile error at probabilities {.025,.05,.25,.5,.75,.95,.975} at most 0.20 standard deviations; unconditional 90% and 95% interval coverage within 0.015 of nominal. Report Monte Carlo standard errors or binomial Wilson intervals. Apply the plug-in-variance gate separately from the oracle gate.

A claim that all-site approximation is adequate requires all 57 sites to pass, while the bridge-specific claim requires all 12 menu members plus at least 99% joint validity and simultaneous Gaussian-rectangle coverage within 0.02 of nominal. Also show the fraction of sites passing and worst-site deviation at each scale; do not discard difficult sites. Conditional shape diagnostics become descriptive only when failures exceed the validity gate. Simultaneous rectangle coverage can use independence and the exact Gaussian maximum threshold, provided simulated bridge experiments are actually independent.

## Decision benchmark and regret

Use a fixed menu, source prior, decoder, target weighting, and interval formula for every selection policy. For budget B enumerate all subsets of 12 designs. Define target loss per replication as the unweighted average across the 17 targets of squared prediction error relative to the known simulation truth. If another target weighting is used, freeze it before simulation.

The Bernoulli exhaustive benchmark chooses the subset minimizing training-pilot mean loss and is evaluated on an independent test simulation. Test outcomes may be shared across policies for paired Monte Carlo comparisons. Report signed excess test MSE = test MSE(policy) - test MSE(training-selected oracle), and relative excess divided by the oracle test MSE. A negative difference is possible because the pilot oracle is a noisy selection rule; never clip it to zero or call it the true mathematical optimum. Report training minimum and test risk separately. Test Monte Carlo uncertainty should use paired per-replication loss differences.

The uniform-random comparator can average the test risks over all fixed-size subsets, giving the exact uniform-subset expectation conditional on test draws. It must not choose the best random draw. Gaussian greedy, Gaussian exhaustive, target-variance greedy, and nearest-profile policies must all use the same posterior update and prior information mapping as the Bernoulli oracle. Oracle noise and source-estimated design noise are separate analyses; bridge outcome plug-in variances cannot leak into a supposedly preselected design.

## Coverage and interpretation

If observed source summaries and V0 are frozen, reported target interval coverage is conditional on those source quantities. It does not establish unconditional repeated-source frequentist coverage, and it may reflect empirical-prior shrinkage bias in addition to Gaussian approximation error. Source-only variance is permissible for prospective selection; exact DGP variance can be a labelled oracle design sensitivity. Keep actual Bernoulli bridge outcomes in the evaluation step.

Keep DGP A approximation error separate from DGP B structural error. For site heterogeneity, target truth must use each target's actual simulated site perturbation, not the common-effect surface. Moderate nonzero residual structural risk may persist under increasing bridge sample size and is not evidence that the central limit approximation itself failed. The sex-category stability assumption and retrospective target-profile access must remain explicit.

## Exact moment identities and a limitation of the MSE comparison

With fixed positive cell counts, the extended probability MLE is K_tc/n_tc. The standardized ATE is therefore a linear combination of independent binomial counts. Its expectation equals the stipulated site truth and its oracle variance equals the displayed Delta variance exactly at finite sample size. The asymptotic step concerns the normal distributional approximation and finite-logit regularity, not this first-moment or variance identity. Restricting to finite-logit successes changes the distribution and must not silently replace the unconditional moment calculations.

For each cell E[qhat(1-qhat)/n] = (1-1/n) q(1-q)/n. Thus downward bias of plug-in variance is expected when cells are small, even without numerical fitting error. An n=1 cell has identically zero plug-in variance and necessarily lacks an interior finite-logit MLE.

For a fixed subset and fixed outcome-independent observation variances, a Gaussian posterior mean is affine in bridge ATE observations. Its target squared risk depends only on their mean and covariance. Consequently the Bernoulli risk and the moment-matched Gaussian risk coincide exactly when those first two moments match, even if the Gaussian distributional approximation is poor. Reported MSE agreement alone therefore cannot establish normality or justify Gaussian interval coverage. Absolute error, tail coverage, finite-MLE failures, and joint diagnostics remain informative.

As a numerical audit, compute the exact subset risk of an affine target predictor a+H y as mean_target[(a+H mu-tau_true)^2] + trace(H Sigma H')/17. This identity can verify Monte Carlo test risk while the requested train/test exhaustive Bernoulli benchmark remains an independently selected evaluation policy. It ceases to suffice unchanged if variances or the decoder adapt to bridge outcomes, if the posterior mean is clipped, or if failures are dropped.

## Root-frozen decisions before numerical execution

Main future cell allocations use Hamilton largest remainders at each arm total scale*n_st, targeting the same population profile p_s. The profile remains the fixed standardization estimand and integer allocation deviations are reported. Historical category-arm zeros are not simply multiplied. The main 12-candidate menu is chosen from outcome-free designs supporting every active category in both arms at all five scales, using lexicographic start and deterministic farthest-first geometry. The same candidate identities are retained for secondary scenarios, even if a completion makes a required cell unavailable. This prespecified support restriction is disclosed; all 57 historical site designs remain in estimator diagnostics, so the selected menu is not evidence of all-site regularity.

DGP calibration uses all source outcomes: unknown-category observations enter a uniform-mixture likelihood. The raw source probability fit supplies planning variances; a declared half-count regularized fit generates DGP probabilities only. The corresponding conditional scientific assumptions remain explicit. Moderate DGP B perturbs the fixed site treatment-response probabilities, with actual target truth using site-specific probabilities.

The base Gaussian prior is mean zero and covariance I_3. Source summaries are regenerated in each Monte Carlo replicate, using three pooled category-specific RD observations with identity regressors. Future bridge draws are independent of source draws, even when their historical design identifiers overlap. The source planning probabilities fitted to real source responses remain fixed, and neither future bridge outcomes nor regenerated source outcomes adapt the planning noise. The analytic risk oracle includes both regenerated-source and future-bridge variance and the appropriate source-mean discrepancy in DGP B.

There are 46 frozen conditions: 10 main conditions with 5,000 evaluation replicates each and 36 secondary conditions with 2,000 each. A separate 2,000-replicate pilot selects each empirical exhaustive Monte Carlo oracle; the selected subset is evaluated in the independent main stream. A separate exact analytic expected-risk oracle is also reported. Pilot minima and held-out risks are distinguished.

The frozen strict parameter adequacy gate includes finite-logit success at least .99. The functional extended-estimator gate requires availability at least .99, absolute standardized mean error at most .10, empirical/oracle variance ratio in [.90,1.10], absolute skewness at most .30, absolute excess kurtosis at most .50, maximum quantile deviation on {.025,.05,.25,.5,.75,.95,.975} at most .20 SD, and both 90% and 95% unconditional coverage within .015 of nominal. Oracle and plug-in variance gates are separate. The full candidate-vector gate requires joint validity at least .99, independent-normal simultaneous rectangle coverage within .02 of nominal, and maximum absolute off-diagonal standardized correlation at most .05. All 57 sites and all 12 candidates are retained in their respective denominators. Strict and functional adequacy are reported separately.

Because true gamma is fixed, the working Gaussian posterior covariance does not automatically provide frequentist coverage, even with regenerated sources. Empirical interval coverage, bias, and source/bridge sampling variance must be interpreted using the repeated-source formulas rather than assuming that the theorem's Bayesian posterior coverage applies.
