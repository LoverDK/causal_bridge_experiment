# Matched weighting on saved Appendix P worlds

The saved experiment supports a substantial weighting benefit in some declared synthetic cells, with important failures elsewhere. This reanalysis adds surface-resolved metrics and Monte Carlo intervals; it creates no new worlds, observations, fits or optimizations. All four weighting rules share each world's calibration, proxies, source-specific trial streams, candidate menu, acquisition prefix, certificate, tolerance and available budget. The independent unit is the world, not a CSV row: 1,800 worlds total, 600 per geometry and 200 per geometry/surface.

The comparison isolates optimized, uniform, inverse-noise-variance and nearest-proxy-source weights with the same minimum geometric envelope. Random versus nearest-proxy acquisition order, R2 versus Rinf certificate, and optimized versus simple weights are separate factors. This is not a comparison against ExAtlas or direct target experimentation. SLSQP plus feasible candidates yields a locally optimized feasible rule, without a certified global optimum. All weights are design-measurable; inverse variance uses known DGP noise scales. R2 here does not permit selecting weights from the evaluation outcomes. The finite-horizon R2/Rinf probability allocations are those of the saved protocol; differences across them should not be described as a pure same-coefficient algebraic penalty comparison.

## What the saved evidence says

On the bridgeable menu, random order, R2 and tolerance .30, optimized weights release 600/600 targets at a mean actual cost of 159.68 new participants; uniform weights release 122/600 (20.33%) at 283.52. The release gain is 79.67 percentage points and participant savings are 123.84. The exact original-seed bootstrap intervals remain [77.00,82.33] percentage points and [119.36,128.32] participants; they are preserved in `original_seed_paired_intervals.csv` rather than silently replaced. The new frozen-seed extension gives [76.83,82.34] and [119.20,128.16], consistent within finite bootstrap Monte Carlo variation. These are exploratory individual percentile intervals, not simultaneous superiority claims.

Surface stratification matters. At this same stopped-policy comparison, optimized weights release every target on each surface, whereas uniform weights release 100/200 linear, 22/200 quadratic and 0/200 sinusoidal targets. Optimized mean participant costs are 132.96, 153.12 and 192.96, respectively; uniform costs are 274.56, 288 and 288. Uniform released-only MAE is undefined in the sinusoidal cell. Every surface, geometry, certificate, order, tolerance and budget remains in the machine-readable outputs; no cell was removed because it was unfavorable.

At a complete prefix of 288 actual participants, optimized versus uniform all-target MAE is .033389 versus .140518 and certificate width is .422440 versus .706055. The paired MAE difference is -.107129, with fresh-seed 95% interval [-.112159,-.102090]; the width difference is -.283615 [-.289532,-.277435]. Fixed-final releases are 600/600 versus 120/600 (20.00%), not 20.33%. The two fewer uniform releases demonstrate that adding a noisy source can worsen simple weighting. Stopped release rates cannot be attached to complete-prefix widths or errors.

The negative cells remain central. Under nearest-proxy order at tolerance .20, all four R2 weighting rules refuse all bridgeable targets. At .30, optimized R2 releases all 600 but every simple rule releases none; mean costs are 241.60 versus 288. Under random order and tolerance .20, optimized R2 releases only 231/600, versus 8/600 uniform and zero for the other two rules. Under random Rinf at .30, optimized releases only 191/600; the simple rules release none. Unreachable menus and supported geometry are retained in every output and must not be pooled away when interpreting usefulness.

The minimum-of-two geometric refinement must also be distinguished from weighting. An independent check of all saved stopped rows finds no release or cost difference between optimized minimum and optimized barycentric-only variants. These weighting results therefore do not establish an additional release/cost gain from the minimum envelope. Nor does fixed random/nearest acquisition validate the log-determinant theorem or demonstrate an outcome-adaptive acquisition policy.

## Metric and interval definitions

`metrics_all_caps.csv` contains 3,072 rows, covering all four caps (0,96,192,288 participants), all 3×3 geometry/surface cells and equal-surface pooled results, both orders, both certificates, both tolerances, four weights and two regimes. Each surface cell uses 200 worlds; pooled geometry cells use 600. Counts and denominators accompany the metrics.

- `first_release_stopped` reproduces the actual policy: stop at the first certificate radius at most the tolerance, carry its output forward, otherwise exhaust the cap. `participants` averages actual cost over every target, including exhausted refusals. `first_release_cost_conditional` averages first-release cost among released targets and is undefined for zero releases; it does not remove failures from the unconditional cost metric.
- `complete_prefix` uses the observations genuinely saved for each full common prefix, even if a policy would have stopped earlier. Its actual participant count is exactly the cap. The certificate release event is evaluated at that prefix, not whether it ever released earlier. First-release cost is inapplicable and missing.
- `all_target_mae` scores the stored weighted point estimate on every target, including refusals; it is a diagnostic estimate when the policy refuses. `released_mae` scores the selected released cohort only. Different methods can release different cohorts, so a difference between their released MAEs is not a common-subpopulation treatment effect.
- `certificate_width` is twice the radius. `returned_output_width` is the actual stopped returned interval, including refusal outer regions, which need not be centered on the weighted estimate and need not equal twice the radius. Complete-prefix plans do not preserve refusal-output endpoints, so their returned-output width and output coverage are missing; certificate width is available without inventing those endpoints.
- Stopped `path_coverage` is the saved full returned-output path event through that cap. Complete-prefix `path_coverage` is the intersection of certificate-coverage events through that prefix. These are different output objects and are labeled by regime. Certificate coverage is also reported separately.
- `bad_release` is the joint event release and absolute error above tolerance, over every world. `selective_risk` divides that event by releases, is missing for zero releases, and has no asserted conditional-coverage guarantee. All retained stopped outputs have zero observed bad releases and 100% observed returned-output path coverage; all saved full-prefix certificates cover here. These finite-sample ties do not prove zero population risk, establish validity, or rank the methods. Constant bootstrap samples produce degenerate intervals for these observed ties.

`metric_intervals_cap288.csv` has 9,216 individual metric intervals, and `paired_intervals_cap288.csv` has 6,912 optimized-minus-comparator differences. They cover all surfaces plus pooled strata, geometries, orders, certificates, tolerances and regimes at the terminal 288-person cap. Earlier caps retain complete descriptive metrics. Frozen seed 2026110803 and 2,000 multinomial whole-world resamples are used. The identical world draw is applied to every method and metric within a surface/geometry; pooled draws average the three separately resampled surfaces equally. Ratio metrics recompute numerator and release denominator on each draw; `valid_draws` records the number with defined ratios. No-release ratios remain missing. These intervals quantify Monte Carlo variation across these synthetic worlds, not real-world uncertainty or independent confirmation on new worlds. There is no multiple-comparison adjustment.

## Audit, provenance and reproduction

The source is the read-only experiment-rebuild workspace specified in `protocol.json`; source DGP seed is 2026091801, with 199 calibration archives, four historical sources of 800 participants, six candidate bridges and at most three new trials of 96 participants. The source protocol explicitly labels the experiment post-review attribution on the existing DGP. Historical, calibration, mechanism-audit and deployment costs are excluded from `participants`.

The reanalysis read all 345,600 stopped records and all 172,800 saved plans, retaining 230,400 stopped and 230,400 full-prefix rows for the four weighting rules. All 14,400 world/order/stage prefixes agree across variants, with no simplex failures. Raw-record and decompressed-plan SHA256 agree with the original manifest. The R2 optimized random and nearest records match all 28,800 overlapping workflow rows exactly in eight numerical/decision fields. An independent script compared 2,304 surface/cap rows against the original stopped and fixed cell summaries; maximum discrepancy is below 5e-12, consistent with saved CSV rounding. The eight original source/config/test/protocol hashes also match. Checks establish arithmetic, pairing and provenance, not the scientific truth of assumptions.

Frozen protocol, source and artifact hashes, package versions and elapsed time are in `protocol.json`, `manifest.json`, `audit_checks.json` and `independent_summary_checks.json`. The main analysis completed in 46.20 seconds on CPU, versus the advance forecast of 60–240 seconds based on a previous 4.14-second audit plus additional bootstrap arithmetic; the independent comparison took about two seconds. No GPU was used. To reproduce, run `python analyze.py`, then `python verify_saved_summaries.py`, then `python finalize_manifest.py` from this directory. The source remains read-only; these commands write only this output directory.
