## 3. Radius, target region, units and geometry

For a saved simplex weight a, proxy centers c, uncertainty radii r and target(c*,r*), the implemented geometric bounds are:

```
Bbar = L*||sum(a_i*c_i)-c*|| + L*(r*+sum(a_i*r_i))
       + H/4*sum_ij a_i*a_j*(||c_i-c_j||+r_i+r_j)^2
BLip = L*sum_i a_i*||c_i-c*|| + L*(r*+sum(a_i*r_i))
R = min(Bbar,BLip) + sampling_allowance(a).
```

The i=j pair distance is set to zero. Uncertainty affects both the direct L-radius term and inflated pairwise curvature; deleting only the former does not remove U. Same-weight comparisons hold the estimate and noise fixed; separately reoptimized arms additionally change weights. Multistart SLSQP supplies feasible saved weights, without a global-optimum certificate.

The fixed-budget certificate interval is weighted-estimate±R and has full width2R. The actual first-release output uses that interval when released; upon refusal it returns a separately computed Lipschitz outer envelope. That envelope need not be centered at the weighted estimate and is not generally the sharp target region. Correct L, not H, justifies the refusal envelope; this is why H changes alone cannot alter its endpoints at an identical source prefix. Different stop prefixes can still change the final returned interval. Empty/inconsistent is not zero-width success; unbounded must remain explicit.

In this synthetic normalized coordinate system, r has mechanism-distance units, L has effect per mechanism-distance units, H has effect per squared mechanism-distance units, and R/delta/noise/interval widths have effect units. All constants, uncertainty sets, effect normalization and the norm must be fixed together. For uniform coordinate rescaling z=c*m(c>0), transporting U and using r'=c*r,L'=L/c,H'=H/c^2 preserves each term. Arbitrary coordinate scaling does not preserve the Euclidean geometry with unchanged L/H. It requires the transported metric/sets and transformed bounds; anisotropic scaling turns balls into ellipsoids, and replacing them by balls can add conservatism. This is a mathematical accounting statement, not a new empirical invariance test. No units/rescaling stress run is present.

The latest paper already states the scientific-unit/versioned-scale requirement near line803 and requires mu on a containing convex domain. The barycenter is a mathematical interpolation point; it need not be a deployable intervention. For physically nonconvex scientific sets, using H requires a justified extension to the required interpolation domain. Without that justification the curvature argument is unsupported. The finite-known-location metric Lipschitz model gives a sharp LP fallback under its own assumptions; unknown locations/curvature/general nonconvex U require a global solver or verified outer relaxation, as stated near line580. The present ablations use a convex square and ball/domain intersections, so they do not experimentally validate arbitrary nonconvex mechanism sets.

Support and curvature have different roles: target-to-barycenter mismatch penalizes unsupported transport; the pairwise H term penalizes interpolation across dispersed sources even when the barycenter matches the target. An observed H0 success at the realized saved weights does not remove the domain-wide interpolation assumption. No real mechanism-coordinate/source/unit audit is supplied by these synthetic runs.

## 5. New-experiment priority decision

Do not rerun L/H, U deletion, two-bound ablation, random Gaussian logdet-versus-exact, or the diagonal anisotropy example. They are already implemented with meaningful negative outcomes.

A narrow computational gap was the relationship between **target-variance greedy and exhaustive target-variance design** at small budgets. Existing PanelD had exhaustive target optimum but no target-greedy row; historical/non-Gaussian runs had target greedy but no exhaustive whole-budget comparator. Root approved the saved-design-only reanalysis after this audit. The frozen protocol/input hashes are in `deterministic_protocol.json`; all200 designs were analyzed at B=1,2,4, with no new data or DGP. Computation took4.788 seconds and independent checking9.389 seconds.

At mainB4, target-greedy radius/exhaustive target-optimal radius has mean1.029687, median1.000,90th percentile1.092738 and maximum1.623690. It attains the optimum in118/200 designs, so82 are suboptimal. Against logdet greedy it is narrower in196/200, tied in2 and wider in2. These results give a useful average improvement within the archived Gaussian designs and a concrete finite-budget limitation of one-step greed. They do not supply a general approximation theorem. Fixed condition-stratum results, all adverse cases, raw114,600 subset scores, candidate/step logs and independent audit are in `deterministic_reanalysis01/`.

If a new designed stress is desired after existing-design reanalysis, use a very small frozen Gaussian linear menu with correlated target-relevant directions (including complementary noisy measurements), not many repeats of the diagonal witness. Compare logdet greedy/exhaustive and target-variance greedy/exhaustive at B=1,2,3; report all cells and the possibility that the target heuristic loses. Fix target variance/loading and cost scales when changing nuisance anisotropy so the mechanism of failure is interpretable. No outcome-adaptive-versus-nonadaptive comparison is meaningful under a known Gaussian covariance where posterior covariance depends only on design; an adaptive extension must define unknown parameters/model misspecification and valid inference separately. That extension is a substantive new task and remains unsupported.

The preregistered saved-design runtime forecast was5–20 seconds CPU, based on historical .016 seconds/design for B4. Actual computation was4.788 seconds; no GPU. No correlated counterfactual benchmark was authorized or executed. Nonlinear or outcome-adaptive acquisition remains a substantive gap.
